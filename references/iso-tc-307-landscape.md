# ISO/TC 307 Standards Landscape for BRM

> **Status date: 2026-08-10.** ISO project stages change. Verify the linked official catalogue before using a draft or publication in a control, contract, audit criterion, or regulatory submission.

## Executive status

ISO/TC 307 remains active. The official committee page reports:

- **14 published ISO standards/publications**;
- **25 standards/projects under development**;
- working groups covering foundations, smart contracts, governance, use cases, interoperability, tokenization, Web3, and forensics/evidence;
- a joint working group with ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 27 for security, privacy, and identity.

The ISO count includes revisions. A published edition can remain current while its replacement is under development.

ISO/TC 307 is a portfolio, not one certifiable “blockchain standard.” Its publications include:

- **International Standards (ISO)** — normative standards;
- **Technical Specifications (ISO/TS)** — normative technical specifications used where full international consensus may not yet be mature;
- **Technical Reports (ISO/TR)** — informative material;
- **PAS** and projects at AWI, WD, CD, DIS, DTS, or PRF stages.

A draft project should inform BRM research but must not be represented as a published requirement.

## How BRM should use the portfolio

1. **Normative core:** use published ISO and ISO/TS publications as controlled references, with edition and status recorded.
2. **Informative support:** use ISO/TR publications as context and design guidance, not as mandatory certification requirements.
3. **Research watch:** map draft projects to BRM backlog items, but label their stage and avoid copying draft requirements into the normative branch.
4. **Revision control:** monitor ISO 22739, ISO 23257, and ISO/TS 23258 because all three foundational publications are being revised.
5. **Crosswalk, not duplication:** BRM should allocate responsibility across components and actors; ISO documents should supply terminology, architecture, governance, security, privacy, interoperability, and audit concepts.

## Certification and regulatory interpretation

The present ISO/TC 307 portfolio does **not** establish a single generic management-system certification for a “blockchain platform.” An organization may obtain certification or assurance against other applicable schemes (for example, ISO/IEC 27001) and use ISO/TC 307 publications as architecture or control guidance. Licensing remains activity- and jurisdiction-specific.

Any future claim such as “ISO/TC 307 certified” must identify:

- the exact standard and edition;
- whether the document is suitable for certification;
- the certification or assurance body;
- scope, sites, services, exclusions, and validity;
- whether the claim concerns an organization, management system, product, or technical component.
